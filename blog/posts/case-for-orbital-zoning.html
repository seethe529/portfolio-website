The Case for Orbital Zoning: Structuring Humanity's Shared Space

October 30, 2025 Analysis 6 min read
Orbital Zoning Framework Space Sustainability Governance LEO MEO GEO Cislunar Mars OZF

The Crowded Skies Above Earth

Low Earth Orbit (LEO) is becoming an increasingly crowded and contested domain. The number of active satellites has surged past tens of thousands, with more being launched every month. Alongside this rapid expansion, the frequency of satellite conjunctions, or close approaches between orbiting objects, continues to rise, heightening the risk of collision and cascading debris events.

⸻

Why It Matters

These risks extend far beyond the aerospace sector. A single major collision can disrupt global communications, weather monitoring, navigation systems, and even financial transactions. The average person might not think twice about what happens in orbit until a viral video shows a fiery reentry streaking across the night sky or a film dramatizes a catastrophic debris chain reaction. But these scenarios aren't science fiction anymore; they are warnings from reality.

⸻

A Shared Domain of Responsibility

If we want to protect this critical domain, we need to treat it as what it is: a shared and collective environment, not one owned by any single nation. It must be managed with the same intentionality and stewardship that we apply to our terrestrial environments. The solution begins with segmentation.

⸻

Extending Boundaries Beyond Earth

Just as we divide land into tracts, parcels, and zones to organize cities and nations, we can extend this logic beyond the atmosphere. By implementing a structured orbital zoning system, what I call the Orbital Zoning Framework (OZF), we can introduce order to the chaos above our heads.

⸻

Scaling the Framework: From LEO to GEO

While the most visible challenges are unfolding in Low Earth Orbit, the same principles apply throughout the orbital environment. Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) hosts critical navigation systems like GPS, Galileo, and GLONASS, while the geostationary belt (GEO) remains the backbone of global communications. Each of these regions faces its own form of congestion and spectrum pressure. A unified zoning framework can extend across these layers, ensuring coordination and sustainability from the surface to the edge of Earth's gravitational influence.

⸻

A Blueprint for the Future

In the future, this same framework could be adapted for cis-lunar space and even Martian orbit, laying the groundwork for interplanetary governance and responsible expansion of human presence beyond Earth.

⸻

A Familiar Analogy

Think of it as the orbital equivalent of the U.S. Census Bureau's TIGER/Line tracts: spatial boundaries that help us analyze, manage, and plan for human activity. In space, these tracts would define specific zones of altitude, inclination, and right ascension, creating a transparent and data-driven architecture for tracking and coordinating satellite operations.

⸻

From Chaos to Coordination

As humanity becomes a truly spacefaring civilization, our governance systems must evolve accordingly. Establishing orbital zones is not just a technical step; it is an ethical one. It signals that we understand our shared responsibility for the space environment, that we value sustainability, accountability, and the rule of law beyond Earth's surface.

The Orbital Zoning Framework represents that first step, from chaos to coordination, from exploitation to stewardship.
